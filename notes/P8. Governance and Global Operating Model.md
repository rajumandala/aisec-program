# P8. Governance and Global Operating Model

P7 moved trust away from a convincing voice and towards a controlled process. The broker now needs the same discipline inside the company. Its India trading team wants an order assistant, its EU subsidiary wants a support copilot, and its US office wants a research summariser. Each says someone else approved the model. None can say who may approve the use.

## The idea

**Approve a use, an owner and an operating boundary, not just a model.** One global baseline should travel with every AI feature. A regional team then adds the controls needed for its entity, customers, workers and data. A global approval is not permission to launch everywhere.

These are **proposed company standards, requiring company adoption**, not existing policy. The examples are hypothetical. No committee, control, review or drill described here has been run. There is no claim of compliance or certification. Suggested meeting and review intervals are operating choices, not legal deadlines.

Read this with [P2. Intake and Risk Tiers](P2.%20Intake%20and%20Risk%20Tiers.md), [P3. Metrics and Roadmap](P3.%20Metrics%20and%20Roadmap.md) and [P6. Standards Map](P6.%20Standards%20Map.md). The tier still comes from capabilities, not seniority or geography.

## Step 1: give each decision an owner

The broker's head of operations owns support drafts. The platform team owns the gateway. Neither owns whether a support agent may send investment advice in a particular market. That needs a local business and compliance decision.

| Decision | Accountable authority | Who supplies the evidence | Limit on the authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purpose, users, outcomes and funding | Named business owner | Product and operations | Cannot waive law or another entity's restrictions |
| Technical implementation and first-line controls | Engineering owner | Product, platform, data owners | Cannot validate its own work as independent assurance |
| Global security baseline and tier rules | CISO or delegated security policy owner | AI security, platform and second-line risk | Cannot declare a use legally permitted |
| Local launch and legal applicability | Local entity's authorised executive, relying on legal/compliance advice | Regional legal, privacy, compliance and product | Global approval does not replace local clearance |
| Residual risk within delegated appetite | Business executive named in the authority schedule | Security/risk assessment and control owners | Acceptance must be bounded, dated and lawful |
| Above-appetite risk or cross-entity dispute | Executive risk committee; board where reserved | Affected entities and second line | Cannot override a prohibition; pause affected scope pending decision |
| Emergency suspension | On-call incident commander, SOC or named regional operator | Alert, incident or suspected harm | Stop first within mandate; restart needs the release authority and fresh evidence |

A job title is not an authority schedule. Before adoption, the company must name delegates, alternates, financial/exposure limits and escalation routes. An unfilled authority field means the affected use cannot launch.

### A ready-to-use authority record

| Field | Fill in before approval |
|---|---|
| Decision ID; feature ID; version | Stable references, not a model nickname |
| Legal entity; markets; affected users | Include workers and people whose data is used |
| Decision sought and alternatives | Approve / redesign / restrict / defer / retire |
| Worst case and exposure ceiling | Amount, accounts, records, destinations, duration |
| Decision maker and written delegation | Name, role, alternate and authority reference |
| Required clearances | Security, local compliance/legal, privacy, responsible AI, independent validation as applicable |
| Evidence and dissent | Versioned links; unresolved findings and reviewers' objections |
| Conditions and acceptance expiry | Scope, owner, review trigger, expiry and what happens at expiry |
| Decision and timestamp | Signer, rationale, permitted regions, excluded uses |

## Step 2: use three lines without moving ownership

Three lines means different jobs, not three sequential signatures on every prompt.

| Line | Broker/MNC roles | Owns | Must not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| First: delivers and operates | Business owner, engineering, platform, data owners, embedded security champions | Design, implementation, tests, user support, monitoring, incident action and fixes | Call self-testing independent validation |
| Second: sets policy and challenges | Risk, compliance, privacy oversight, responsible AI and independent security/model-risk reviewers | Standards, challenge, oversight, escalation and validation where mandated | Build a control and then independently approve that same control |
| Third: independent assurance | Internal audit, reporting through its audit governance route | Audit whether governance and controls work, including the first two lines | Become the routine release approver or accept product risk |

An AI security engineer may help build a test and also explain policy. For that release, a different qualified reviewer must supply any required independent challenge. Organisational labels alone do not establish independence. Record reporting line, prior involvement, conflicts, access to evidence and freedom to report adverse results. If a small team cannot separate these jobs, arrange a qualified cross-team or external reviewer with an independent reporting route; do not rename self-review.

### A small RACI for the support copilot

A = accountable for this activity; R = does the work; C = consulted; I = informed. This table allocates work, not a right to bypass the authority table.

| Activity | Business owner | Engineering/platform | Second-line reviewers | Local legal/compliance | Internal audit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Define purpose and permitted use | A | R | C | C | I |
| Implement and operate controls | A | R | C | C | I |
| Deliver independent validation | C | C | A/R, independent of build | C | I |
| Record legal applicability | C | C | C | A/R | I |
| Authorise local launch | A, local delegated executive | R, deploy only approved scope | C | C, clearance required | I |
| Audit the programme | C | C | C | C | A/R |

## Step 3: one baseline, regional execution

The proposed model is **federated**: central services and minimum controls, local responsibility for use. A regional requirement can tighten the baseline. A conflict is escalated, not silently resolved by picking the easier rule.

| Layer | Centrally owned | Locally owned | Evidence at handoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inventory and tier | Common feature IDs, intake schema, tier calculation | Entity, purpose, users, language, local data sources | Shared record with regional annex |
| Platform | Approved snapshots, scoped identity, egress controls, audit schema | Allowed regional endpoints and support access | Tested routing and access rules, not a dropdown screenshot alone |
| Legal/privacy | Common questions and contract templates | Applicability, terms, notices, transfers, retention and required assessments | P10 obligations record and written local clearance |
| Responsible AI | Common impact questions and escalation criteria | Local harms, language, accessibility, appeal and vulnerable-user needs | Impact assessment and local evaluations |
| Operations | Common incident severity and change process | Staffed hours, local escalation, customer remedy and regional kill authority | Named on-call, handover and tested fallback |
| Assurance | Minimum review scope and independence rules | Qualified reviewers and local evidence | Validation report, findings and closure record |

A shared regional annex should contain: legal entity; accountable executive; users and exclusions; languages; hosting, inference, retrieval, logs, backup and support locations; approved subprocessors; retention and holds; local obligations; required assessments; support hours; emergency contacts; fallback; permitted model/configuration versions; clearance and review triggers.

**No hidden export through operations.** A local inference endpoint does not prove that traces, support tickets, administrator access or backups stay local. Map those flows separately. Region failover is disabled unless the destination and its access, terms and controls have been cleared.

## Step 4: decide only what needs a decision

The committee in P3 should settle policy conflicts and material exceptions, not review every feature. Routine releases use delegated authority and the gates in P9.

| Situation | Action under this proposal | Decision owner |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 0, known purpose, no local assessment trigger | Register and follow standard gates; no bespoke deep security review | Delegated product owner |
| Tier 3 order assistant within approved market | Deep review, independent validation and bounded risk decision before release | Delegated business executive plus required clearances |
| EU team wants to reuse the India approval | Reuse technical evidence; assess local applicability, language, data and operations | EU entity's delegated executive |
| Vendor cannot disable cross-region support access | Keep affected data/use off; seek contractual/technical alternative | Local owner with legal/privacy and security |
| Security exception conflicts with law | No risk acceptance can authorise it; redesign or exclude scope | Legal/compliance advice to authorised executive |
| Acceptance expires or supporting evidence changes | Block expansion; pause affected use unless renewed lawfully through the defined process | Existing authority, not an auto-renewal script |
| Suspected ongoing data leak | Suspend affected tools/keys and preserve minimised evidence | Incident commander; notify regional owners |

A valid exception names the unmet control, reason, affected scope, worst case, compensating control and test, funding owner, expiry and shutdown condition. It never changes a legal duty. Unresolved dissent goes into the decision record; the reviewer is not asked to turn a red finding green because management accepted a bounded risk.

## Step 5: connect security to responsible AI

Security asks whether a poisoned ticket can expose another client's account. Responsible AI asks whether the copilot treats some clients worse, invents advice, excludes a language group or leaves a person with no way to challenge an outcome. Privacy asks whether the processing is justified and minimised. Compliance asks whether the activity is permitted. These questions share an inventory, not an answer.

For each use, record: intended benefit; affected people; foreseeable misuse; material harms; fairness/quality measures by relevant group and language where lawful; accessibility; uncertainty; disclosure; escalation to a person; correction/appeal; owner; evidence and limits. Route employment, credit, financial advice and other consequential uses for specialist assessment even if the P2 security tier is low. A legally required assessment is determined by applicability, not this example list alone.

**Worked example:** the broker removes links from support drafts. That alone does not remove the P2 trifecta: plaintext, wrong recipients, images and other attacker-usable routes must also be bounded or eliminated. Any tier reduction requires checking all current P2 facts, including consequential-use triggers. The draft still tells Ravi that a rejected withdrawal has no appeal. Security's egress test can pass while customer outcomes fail. Product must add an accurate escalation route, operations must staff it, and responsible-AI/compliance reviewers must test misleading answers and uneven language quality. A lower security tier does not close that finding.

## Step 6: make governance observable

Add these proposed measures to P3, with owners and denominators:

- Active entity/use combinations with current local clearance, divided by all active combinations.
- Required independent validations complete for the deployed version, divided by those required; show overdue findings separately.
- Expired exceptions still in use; unresolved cross-region control conflicts.
- Regions with a staffed emergency stop and a completed drill; untested is not passed.
- Customer/worker challenges, corrected outcomes and time to human resolution, split where useful and lawful.

A blank dashboard is more honest than a green one built from planned tests.

## OWASP risks in this note

The categories follow the course's 2026 OWASP naming. These are hypothetical failure paths, not observed findings.

- **LLM02: Sensitive Information Disclosure.** Example: regional hosting is approved but global support can read raw traces. Issue: location approval omitted access. Fix: map and restrict support access and log flows in the regional annex.
- **LLM03: Excessive Agency.** Example: the global model approval is reused to enable unattended orders. Issue: nobody approved the new capability. Fix: recompute P2 facts and require use-specific authority.
- **ASI03: Identity and Privilege Abuse.** Example: the local operator cannot revoke a global agent key during an incident. Issue: authority exists only on paper. Fix: scoped regional revocation and a drill with an alternate operator.
