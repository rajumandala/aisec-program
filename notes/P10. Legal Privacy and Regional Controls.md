# P10. Legal Privacy and Regional Controls

[P9. Lifecycle and Release Gates](P9.%20Lifecycle%20and%20Release%20Gates.md) asks for local clearance before launch. The broker's EU support team forwards the India assistant's tier 3 approval and asks whether that is enough. It is not. The tier says what the system can reach. It does not say which laws apply, whether personal data may be used for this purpose, or whether an overseas support engineer may read its logs.

## The idea

**Keep legal applicability, security exposure and impact assessment as separate decisions, connected by one inventory ID.** Reuse evidence, not conclusions that depend on a different entity, purpose or region.

These are **proposed company standards requiring adoption**, not legal advice or adopted company policy. Nothing here has been run or legally cleared. Examples are hypothetical and do not assert compliance or certification. Counsel must verify current authoritative law, territorial scope, commencement, transitions and sector-specific requirements for each use. No legal deadline is supplied or inferred here, including from dates in earlier notes.

## Step 1: do not turn a security tier into a legal class

| Use at the broker/MNC | P2 security view | Separate legal/impact question | Proposed decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Staff summary of public exchange circulars, no tools or private data | Could be tier 0 if all P2 facts match | Copyright/licensing, permitted use, inaccurate advice if redistributed, AI disclosure where applicable | Register; screen actual purpose and audience; do not label “unregulated” |
| Client support copilot reading private tickets and rendering external links | Tier 3 by trifecta | Personal data purposes, controller/processor roles, transfers, consumer/financial-service duties, applicable AI transparency | Security deep review plus local legal/privacy assessment |
| CV ranking from public profiles, answer-only internal service | Tier 3 under P2's consequential-use trigger; verify actual purpose and facts | Employment, discrimination, personal-data and AI-system classification may be material | Specialist review; neither tier nor public source settles legal permission |
| Tool assessing an individual's creditworthiness | Tier 3 under P2's consequential-use trigger; also check access, data and powers | Could fall within a legally defined consequential/high-risk category where relevant law applies; assess exceptions and role | Counsel records classification and duties; never inherit the order assistant's conclusion |
| Unattended trading mandate | Tier 3 because it acts alone | Local brokerage, suitability/advice, authorisation, record-keeping and customer-remedy duties | Domain compliance review in every affected market |

“High risk” in a statute is a defined legal category, not P2's tier 3. A system may be one, both or neither. Passing security tests cannot authorise unlawful processing. A lawful use can still have an unacceptable security exposure.

## Step 2: decide applicability from facts

Start at G0 with a screen; complete the analysis before the gate that would introduce the affected data or use. Unknown is a status, not an exemption.

| Question | Evidence to collect | Who records the conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| What is the actual purpose and decision? | Task, intended use, exclusions, outputs and who relies on them | Product and local business |
| Which entities, people and markets are affected? | Establishments, customers/workers/data subjects, where offered and where output is used | Regional legal/compliance |
| Which legal roles does each party hold? | Who determines purposes/means; who builds, brands, deploys or materially modifies; contracts and actual behaviour | Legal/privacy; roles assessed per activity |
| Which data and flows exist? | Collection source, sensitivity, inference, retrieval, logs, access, support, subprocessors, backups and training | Engineering, data owner and vendor management |
| Which regimes may apply? | Privacy, AI-specific, financial services, employment, consumer, IP, confidentiality, outsourcing and cybersecurity requirements | Relevant counsel/compliance specialists |
| What triggers duties or exclusions? | Precise provisions, conditions, exceptions, territorial scope and commencement | Legal/compliance with authoritative sources |
| What changes the answer? | New region, purpose, role, autonomy, personal data, vendor terms, law or deployment context | Named obligation owner |

Screen explicitly for referral to the right specialist where the use involves employment or works councils, biometrics, children or other vulnerable people, accessibility, export controls or sanctions, IP or trade secrets, consumer or sector-specific rules, and legal holds. These are referral triggers, not assertions that a particular duty applies; record the factual scope and specialist conclusion in the register.

Contract labels do not settle legal roles. The broker may be a deployer for one activity and have provider obligations for another, depending on the facts and law. Buying a model does not automatically transfer the broker's duties to the provider. A global master agreement does not by itself clear every subsidiary's use.

## Step 3: maintain an obligations register

Use one row per duty and applicable scope, linked to P6's controls and P9's gate record. The register is a decision tool, not a copied list of laws.

| Ready-to-use field | Required entry |
|---|---|
| Obligation ID; feature ID; version | Link the duty to deployed scope |
| Entity, region and purpose | Include affected people and excluded uses |
| Regime and authoritative source | Provision, official URL/document, version, source retrieval date |
| Applicability and role | Applies / does not apply / uncertain, factual reasoning, conditions and reviewer |
| Effective/transition dates | Verified dates and source, or unknown; distinguish legal date from internal target |
| Required action | Specific outcome, not “comply with privacy” |
| Accountable owner and implementer | Named business/control owner and delivery team |
| Controls and evidence | Control IDs, configuration/test/contract/notice evidence, limits |
| Assessments and dependencies | DPIA, transfer assessment, impact assessment, supplier approval, specialist review |
| Status and gaps | Not assessed / in progress / implemented-unverified / verified / blocked; findings |
| Gate consequence | Which region/use remains off until which evidence exists |
| Legal review and next trigger | Reviewer, decision date, change triggers and internal review target |
| Incident/rights linkage | Escalation route and any verified duty to respond/report, with trigger and calculation owner |

**Illustrative rows — not legal determinations:**

| ID | Scope and issue | Applicability status | Proposed control/evidence | Gate consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OBL-EU-01 | EU entity's support-ticket processing; privacy purpose and transparency | Pending privacy/legal assessment | Data map, purpose/lawful-basis analysis where required, notice and approved access rules | No pilot with affected personal data while mandatory grounds/conditions are unresolved |
| OBL-XFER-02 | Remote support outside approved region | Uncertain pending full access/subprocessor map | Transfer mechanism and assessments where required, access restrictions and tests | Keep that access disabled pending clearance |
| OBL-IN-03 | India order assistant; brokerage and record-keeping duties | Pending local compliance determination | Applicable rule citations, exact order audit trail, retention decision and retrieval test | No live order use until required obligations are met |
| OBL-AI-04 | Proposed CV-ranking use in EU entity | Pending role/classification assessment | Intended-use facts, current official AI-law analysis and other employment/privacy review | No employment deployment while required classification/conditions remain open |

Do not write “verified” because a vendor has a certificate or a contract was signed. Identify which requirement the evidence supports and what remains untested. Dates and reporting clocks belong here only after verification. The incident runbook must distinguish legal clocks, contractual commitments and internal service targets; counsel/compliance determine the trigger and calculation, and operations record the actual timestamps.

## Step 4: run a DPIA workflow, not a form exercise

A **data protection impact assessment (DPIA)** assesses privacy risks from processing. Whether a DPIA is legally required, what it must include and whether consultation is required depends on the applicable law. A company can also choose an internal assessment for uses below a legal threshold; label that choice correctly. A security threat model and responsible-AI impact assessment contribute evidence but do not replace a legally required DPIA.

| Stage | Work and ready-to-use outputs | Owner / decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Screen | Feature/purpose, people, data categories, scale, monitoring/profiling, new technology, sensitive data, combination of sources and consequential decisions; applicable screening criteria | Product supplies facts; privacy records required / internal assessment / not required with reasons |
| 2. Describe | Collection-to-deletion flow; parties/roles; sources; volumes; recipients; locations; support; training; data/derived-data retention | Engineering and data owner; validate vendor claims |
| 3. Justify | Purpose, necessity, proportionality, alternatives, minimisation; lawful basis and additional conditions where relevant; whether less intrusive means meet the need | Business owner with privacy/legal advice |
| 4. Assess people’s risk | Disclosure, exclusion, discrimination, inaccurate inference, surveillance, loss of control, inability to challenge; severity/likelihood and affected groups | Assessment team; seek affected-party input where appropriate/required |
| 5. Treat and verify | Remove fields, reduce scope, disable training, constrain access/egress, human review, notices, correction/appeal, deletion controls; tests and residual gaps | Control owners implement; reviewers challenge evidence |
| 6. Decide | Residual risk, privacy/DPO advice where applicable, dissent, business decision and legal conditions; assess required regulator consultation before proceeding | Accountable controller/entity authority under applicable governance |
| 7. Maintain | Approved version, review triggers, incidents, changes, complaints, supplier/law changes and effectiveness evidence | Named assessment owner; reopen relevant gates |

Where high residual risk triggers a legal consultation requirement, an executive risk acceptance is not a substitute. Hold affected processing until mandatory conditions are met. An internal target date for completing a DPIA is not a statutory deadline.

### Worked example: support tickets across three regions

The broker wants one copilot for India, EU and US support. Tickets contain bank details and identity documents. Drafts have no external links, but the tracing vendor stores full prompts and global administrators can read them.

1. **Screen:** personal-data and impact screening covers all three entities separately. Record whether each requires a DPIA or another assessment; do not assume one jurisdiction's result settles the others.
2. **Describe:** map tickets to retrieval, inference, trace storage, support access, backups and subprocessors. A regional inference URL covers only one arrow.
3. **Minimise:** mask unnecessary bank/identity fields; use permission-aware retrieval; disable raw payload tracing by default and provider training where required by the approved design. Test both normal and error logging.
4. **Separate:** use regional stores, keys and roles; prohibit unapproved failover and restrict remote support. Local notices, purposes, retention and rights handling require review, not a generic global banner.
5. **Assess outcomes:** a draft that incorrectly refuses Ravi's complaint can harm him even without a leak. Responsible-AI/compliance reviewers assess quality, languages, escalation and remedy alongside privacy/security work.
6. **Decide:** an entity can proceed only after its required evidence and conditions are met. If a vendor cannot support the approved restrictions, redesign, choose another vendor or keep that scope off.

**Actual status: design example only; screening, DPIA, transfer review, tests and approvals not run.** No regional launch is authorised by this note.

## Step 5: turn regional decisions into controls

| Concern | Proposed control | Evidence to request/test |
|---|---|---|
| Location versus access | Specify storage, processing and remote-access permissions separately | End-to-end flow and support role/access tests |
| Cross-border processing | Legal review of actual flows and required mechanisms/assessments | Applicable analysis, agreements, subprocessor list and change notifications |
| Model training and reuse | Explicit approved purpose and contractual/settings restrictions | Terms, account settings, audit evidence and stated limits on verification |
| Retention | Schedule per data class, purpose, entity and applicable obligation | Config, lifecycle jobs, samples and exception/hold records |
| Logging and incident response | Minimise payloads; protect necessary evidence; controlled break-glass access | Redaction/error-path tests, access logs and incident evidence handling |
| Language and accessibility | Approved local notices and usable human route | Representative user tests and translation review |
| Vendor outage | Fail closed or use a cleared regional alternative | Forced-failure routing test; no implicit global fallback |
| Subprocessor or terms change | Notice, assessment, register update and gate reopening | Vendor monitoring, owner and tested disable/export path |

“Data residency” is not a synonym for lawful processing or approved transfer. Encryption helps security but does not answer every legal question. Do not advertise “zero retention” unless its scope, exclusions and evidence support that exact claim.

### Vendor review fields

Capture: contracting entity; services and legal roles; data categories/purposes; endpoints and locations; remote access; subprocessors and change process; training/reuse; retention including abuse logs and backups; security controls; audit/evidence rights; incident assistance; rights-request support; deletion/export on exit; liability and restrictions; vendor version/change controls; unresolved limitations; legal/privacy/security reviewers; approved uses and exclusions. A certificate is one scoped piece of evidence, not a release approval.

## Step 6: handle rights, deletion and retention together

A request to delete personal data is not resolved by deleting the original PDF. Copies may exist in indexes, summaries, agent memory, traces, evaluation sets and vendors. Nor should an engineer delete regulated transaction records or evidence under a lawful hold without an approved decision.

Ready-to-use request/deletion workflow:

- [ ] Authenticate the requester proportionately; record the request and route to the responsible entity.
- [ ] Have privacy/legal determine applicable right, scope, exemptions, retention/hold constraints and any verified response clock. Record receipt/trigger timestamps; do not invent a universal deadline.
- [ ] Locate source and derived copies through lineage, including subprocessors, caches, exports, fine-tunes and backups.
- [ ] Decide per copy: delete, correct, restrict, retain under a documented duty/hold, or escalate technical inability. Record reason, authority and access restrictions.
- [ ] Execute approved actions; remove/rebuild affected index entries and summaries; invalidate caches and prevent deleted data re-entering from upstream ingestion.
- [ ] Apply an approved backup plan: restrict access, prevent ordinary reuse, expire under the verified schedule and reapply deletion after restore. Record the limits instead of promising immediate erasure of every backup.
- [ ] Request relevant vendor/subprocessor action and evidence; track outstanding copies and unverifiable claims.
- [ ] Verify with permitted search/retrieval and restore-path tests; record methods, coverage and limitations.
- [ ] Respond through the authorised privacy process with accurate action/retention explanations; close only the resolved scope and track residual tasks.

If content may have been used in training, source deletion does not establish weight-level erasure. Record what is known, seek supplier remediation, evaluate exposure and required action with privacy/legal, and do not claim successful unlearning without evidence. Apply the same copy inventory at P9's retirement gate.

## Step 7: connect records to release and change

At G1, the obligations register identifies required assessments and data boundaries. At G2, it controls pilot data and access. At G3, unresolved mandatory requirements block the affected use/region. At G4, law, purpose, vendor, access and region changes reopen analysis. At G5, it supplies the retained/deleted/restricted decisions.

A clearance record should say: **which entity, purpose, people, release, regions, assumptions, source versions, controls and conditions were assessed, by whom, and what remains unknown.** “Legal approved AI” is not a usable clearance.

## OWASP risks in this note

Using the course's 2026 category names; examples are hypothetical:

- **LLM02: Sensitive Information Disclosure.** Example: local model calls send full tickets to a global tracing vendor. Issue: incomplete flow/applicability analysis. Fix: include telemetry, support and subprocessors in the assessment and tests.
- **LLM04: Supply Chain.** Example: a supplier changes its training terms after approval. Issue: contract review treated as permanent. Fix: change monitoring, register update and reopening affected gates.
- **ASI03: Identity and Privilege Abuse.** Example: global administrators can query a regional client index. Issue: hosting was mistaken for an access boundary. Fix: regional roles, scoped identities and negative access tests.
