# 12. Incident Response and Resilience

At market open, the broker's SOC sees a support agent export unusually many CRM records. A second alert shows an order assistant retrying timed-out orders. The model provider reports no outage. Waiting for the provider to call this an incident would be a decision to let both continue.

## The idea

AI response is the enterprise incident process with additional evidence and containment points: model routes, agent grants, poisoned retrieval and model-generated actions. It is not a separate command structure.

**Stop the harmful capability, preserve what happened, and restore a bounded service—not merely a working chatbot.**

This note proposes playbooks for an MNC broker; nothing has been implemented or exercised here. All severity thresholds, response targets and recovery objectives below are illustrative planning choices, not promises or legal deadlines. The SOC and enterprise incident response team own response. Application, platform, IAM, trading operations, fraud, legal, privacy and continuity teams retain their responsibilities. AI security advises on AI failure modes and tests.

## Step 1: declare on impact, not on model wording

Use the enterprise severity scheme; the table illustrates how AI scenarios could map into it. A credible suspected breach can justify escalation before full confirmation. On uncertainty, take the higher provisional severity and record the evidence needed to revise it.

| Illustrative severity | Trigger and broker example | Initial action and internal target |
|---|---|---|
| SEV1 critical | Suspected unauthorised trading, cross-client data disclosure, privileged supplier-token compromise, or critical business-service tolerance at risk | Page enterprise IR and business duty manager immediately; aim for incident command within 15 minutes, updates every 30 minutes |
| SEV2 high | Confirmed bounded exposure or important degradation without current critical harm; poisoned research index affecting one desk | SOC pages application/platform on-call and IR; aim for named command within 30 minutes, hourly updates |
| SEV3 moderate | Exploit attempt contained by code, no evidence of disclosure/action, limited noncritical disruption | SOC creates investigation ticket; owner acknowledges within one business day, escalate if scope grows |
| Service request / vulnerability | Rude output or leaked public system prompt with no demonstrated impact | Route to product feedback or vulnerability intake; investigate reach rather than dismiss on wording |

Containment must not wait for these targets to expire. Availability, conduct, fraud and privacy impact all count; an accidental agent action can be SEV1 without an attacker.

**Declaration card:** `incident ID / UTC detected and declared times / reporter / provisional severity and reason / affected entities, regions, features and clients / known facts vs hypotheses / current harm / commander and deputy / bridge and restricted evidence store / next update time`.

## Step 2: assign command before the crisis

| Role | Decision or action |
|---|---|
| Enterprise incident commander, with regional deputy | Sets severity, priorities and containment scope; maintains decision log and handover |
| SOC and forensic lead | Triage, scope, timeline, evidence custody and monitoring for continuing activity |
| Application/platform/IAM on-call | Executes feature isolation, model-route block, queue pause, token/grant revocation and credential rotation |
| Trading and business operations | Reconciles orders and client impact; decides manual/degraded service within approved controls |
| Legal, privacy and regulatory compliance | Determine obligations per entity/jurisdiction, preserve legal holds, approve regulatory/privacy notices |
| Communications and client service | Issue approved, factual staff/client statements and support instructions |
| Business continuity and service owner | Activate continuity plan; demonstrate recovery against business tolerances |
| AI security and model/retrieval specialists | Identify injection/persistence paths, affected artifacts, safe tests and remediation evidence |

Preauthorise named on-call roles to isolate a feature and revoke its grants on credible active harm, with prompt commander notification. Emergency authority, scope and backups belong in access policy; a chat message is not an access grant. Market-wide shutdowns follow the existing trading and crisis authority, not an AI engineer's discretion. For follow-the-sun response, hand over commander, open decisions, clocks, evidence links and next actions explicitly; one commander owns the incident at a time.

## Step 3: use a timed first-response checklist

These are proposed elapsed-time checkpoints, not reasons to delay an urgent action.

| Checkpoint | Executable action | Evidence of completion |
|---|---|---|
| First 15 minutes | SOC opens incident, pages owners, records first-known times and preserves alert IDs. On-call isolates affected feature/session, pauses dispatch and revokes authority when harm is credible | Incident ID, page receipt, kill-switch acknowledgement and last successful harmful-action timestamp |
| First 30 minutes | Commander scopes shared model/tool/vendor dependencies and other regions; forensic lead secures logs/config snapshots; operations checks in-flight actions | Dependency list, artifact hashes, grants revoked, unresolved transaction list |
| First hour | Legal/privacy/compliance start obligation assessment in parallel; business owner chooses degraded service; communications sends approved holding update | Notification register, continuity decision, next update time |
| Until contained | Verify downstream action paths are stopped; hunt for copied credentials, secondary agents and persistence; reassess severity each update | Gateway, IAM and destination-system evidence, not just a silent model session |

Contain first if capture would prolong active harm. Record evidence lost and why. A gateway kill switch may stop new inference but not a worker already holding a tool token; disable dispatch, revoke grants and verify at the destination as well. Pause retries until business operations knows which requests already executed.

## Step 4: preserve enough to explain and reproduce

Default traces should remain minimised, as in 01. Do not turn on unrestricted prompt capture across the company during an incident.

| Evidence | Collection rule |
|---|---|
| Identity and actions | Session/trace IDs, pseudonymous actor and delegation chain, grants/scopes, tool inputs/outputs needed for the incident, destination audit events and transaction IDs |
| Runtime state | Model endpoint/snapshot or unresolved alias, provider request IDs, prompt/template version, tool schema, gateway/guardrail policy and deployment/configuration hashes |
| Sources and persistence | Relevant original document/email, retrieval chunk IDs, index/version, memory/summaries, queues, caches, generated artifacts and feedback/eval ingestion records |
| Timeline | UTC timestamps, clock source/skew, collector, acquisition time, source system and any gaps |
| Custody | Case ID, hashes, read-only originals, access log, transfers, retention/hold authority and authorised disposal |

Forensic lead collects a narrow encrypted evidence set with least-privilege access; legal/privacy authorises any exceptional content collection, its scope, duration and regional transfer. Avoid putting client text, tokens or secrets in the incident chat. Request provider preservation promptly with request IDs and time windows; track acknowledgement and retention limits. Absence of full prompts is an investigation limitation, not proof of no leak. Reproduction uses an isolated environment and sanitised copies; never replay a live trading tool call to see what happens.

## Step 5: choose the playbook by capability

| Scenario | Contain now | Scope, eradicate and recover | Exit evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Injection causes client-data export from support | Disable export/send tool and affected sessions; revoke vendor grants; block known destination while hunting other routes | Match CRM reads/exports to identities and clients; remove poisoned sources and derived memory/index entries; patch permissions and egress | Authorised-data tests plus known attack and variants cannot export; destination logs confirm revocation |
| Agent proposes or places wrong/duplicate orders | Stop agent dispatch and mandates; leave approved conventional trading routes available if safe | Trading ops reconciles broker/exchange/order ledgers using idempotency keys; handles cancellation/correction through existing rules, not agent guesses | Every queued/in-flight order classified; no blind replay; hard limits and confirmations retested |
| Compromised MCP package, prompt or model artifact | Quarantine affected version; pause release; revoke exposed credentials and tool access | Software supply-chain team traces affected deployments, replaces from verified source and checks persistence; product reruns evals | Artifact provenance/digest, clean deployment and credential rotation verified |
| Model host or gateway unavailable | Circuit-break requests, cap retries, stop autonomous action | Platform/continuity activates tested manual service or approved fallback with matching data-location terms and feature evals | Bounded service meets recovery objective; no duplicate jobs or hidden backlog |
| Deepfake payout request | Fraud/payment operations holds payment under existing authority; uses known-channel verification | Fraud and finance investigate beneficiary/contact changes and invoke payment recovery process | Two-person approval and authenticated change controls verified; AI security only assists threat analysis |

**Worked injection example:** a malicious ticket tells a support agent to email a CSV of EU clients. The SOC declares provisional SEV1 for suspected cross-client disclosure. Platform removes send/export capability; IAM revokes the connector token; CRM operations verifies exports have ceased. Forensics retains the ticket and relevant trace IDs before the retrieval team removes both indexed chunks and derived summaries. Privacy determines affected people and obligations; support uses its existing non-AI queue. Recovery requires an attack test against a sanitised copy and proof that the agent cannot read clients outside its queue, even if the model follows the instruction.

## Step 6: run notification as a parallel workstream

There is no single global “AI incident deadline”. Requirements vary by legal entity, regulated service, incident type and jurisdiction; some clocks run from awareness or discovery, not final confirmation. Compliance/legal maintain and verify the current obligation register against authoritative rules before deployment and during an incident. 04's India examples are prompts to check the applicable CERT-In, SEBI and exchange duties, not a complete multinational notification guide.

**Notification register fields:** `entity / jurisdiction / regulator, exchange, privacy authority, client or contractual recipient / applicable rule and source/version / trigger and awareness timestamp / timezone / calculated deadline / reporting owner and approver / minimum facts / secure channel / submitted time and receipt / next update deadline / decision not to notify and rationale`.

Legal/privacy/compliance determine applicability; the commander provides facts and does not reset clocks by changing severity. Submit a permitted preliminary report with uncertainties clearly marked when full facts are unavailable, then update it. Communications must distinguish confirmed disclosure from suspected exposure and avoid unsupported “no data lost” claims. Supplier reporting is an input, not a substitute for the broker's own duties.

## Step 7: recover a business service, not just a model endpoint

Business impact analysis sets an overall disruption tolerance. **RTO** is the target elapsed time from disruption to the agreed usable service level. **RPO** is the maximum acceptable age of the recoverable data/state, expressed as potential data loss. A short RTO does not permit restoring poisoned memory, widening access or losing financial records.

The following targets are illustrative and untested. Service owners must fund and validate them; record observed results separately.

| Service / recovery scope | Proposed RTO | Proposed RPO | Recovery mode and acceptance test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Support drafting | 4 hours to usable non-AI support queue | 15 minutes for draft state; authoritative tickets have their own stricter service objective | Staff use CRM directly; original ticket history and permissions intact; backlog within staffed capacity |
| Research summaries | 1 business day to reviewed publication workflow | 24 hours for generated summaries, rebuildable from retained sources | Analysts publish manually; sources/current versions verified; affected index rebuilt cleanly |
| AI order-assistant capability | 2 hours to safe conventional order entry, not autonomous AI restoration | Zero loss of acknowledged orders in the authoritative ledger | No replay of uncertain requests; broker/exchange reconciliation and existing order controls pass |
| Gateway policy and agent configuration | 1 hour to validated restricted configuration | 15 minutes for config state; separately retain immutable audit records | Restore signed/versioned configuration, revoke stale grants, validate routes and deny rules |

“Zero loss” is a business requirement to demonstrate through durable records and reconciliation, not a claim that every backup has zero RPO. If the architecture cannot meet it, report the gap and constrain launch. A manual route must have capacity, trained staff and compatible regional hours; “use the call centre” is not a plan for ten times its normal volume.

Recovery approval requires: root cause or a verified compensating boundary; clean artifacts and indexes; completed secret rotation; queue/ledger reconciliation; functional and adversarial tests; alerting enabled; business owner and commander sign-off with relevant control owners. Use a limited cohort first, monitor explicit stop conditions, then expand. A fallback model must already be approved for this feature, data and jurisdiction. Restoring AI can wait after safe business service has resumed.

## Step 8: prove the playbook and close the gaps

Propose quarterly tier 3 containment drills, twice-yearly cross-region tabletops and an annual end-to-end recovery/exit rehearsal, adjusted by enterprise risk. Include the supplier unavailable, the primary administrator on leave, a stale backup, poisoned derived memory and a deadline falling during regional handover.

Record `scenario / participants / start and stop clocks / expected vs observed RTO/RPO / actions that still succeeded after isolation / missing evidence / notification decisions / reconciliation result / gap owner / due date / retest evidence`. A tabletop proves decisions, not a technical restore. After an incident, hold a blameless review, update threat models and exposure, add regression tests, and track remediation to independent verification rather than closing on a promise.

## Problem and next step

**Problem:** the playbook needs people on call, protected engineering time, evidence storage and tested manual capacity. None comes free, and the people writing a control should not be its only judges.
