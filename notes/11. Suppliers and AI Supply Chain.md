# 11. Suppliers and AI Supply Chain

The broker buys a support assistant in Europe, calls a model API from India and lets a US development team install a coding agent. Procurement sees three purchases. An attacker sees three sets of credentials, several subprocessors and a shared cloud region. Buying the feature does not buy away the broker's responsibility for it.

## The idea

Use the existing third-party risk process, then add the AI-specific questions: what gets sent, what can act, what changes without us knowing, and how do we leave?

**The contract sets the obligation. The architecture limits the damage. A drill shows whether either works.**

This is a proposed operating design, not a description of controls implemented at the broker. All examples, thresholds and timings are illustrative. Procurement, third-party risk, legal, privacy, technology operations and business owners keep their existing jobs. AI security supplies specialist assessment and tests, not a parallel procurement department.

## Step 1: register the relationship, not just the invoice

A free MCP plugin with a production token can be more dangerous than an expensive model used for public text. Include SaaS AI settings, resellers, embedded AI, open-weight models, datasets, retrieval sources, prompt libraries and coding extensions. One supplier can support several features and legal entities; record the links rather than duplicating a questionnaire.

| Supplier record field | Example or required detail |
|---|---|
| Accountable business owner; technical operator; legal entities | Head of client service; support platform team; India and EU subsidiaries |
| Service and feature IDs; 02 tier | Support drafting; tier determined from actual data and actions |
| Data and location | Ticket text, client identifiers, embeddings, logs; processing, storage, backup and support-access countries separately |
| Authority | CRM read scopes, outbound email permission, token issuer, lifetime and revocation procedure |
| Dependency chain | Reseller → model host → base model; cloud/region, vector store, identity provider, subprocessors |
| Approved configuration | Model snapshot, retention/training settings, routes, connector versions, configuration evidence date |
| Evidence and decisions | Assessment ID, findings, contract clauses, risk acceptance owner/expiry, next review trigger |
| Continuity and exit | Business impact, tested manual route, export format, deletion scope, estimated migration time |

Procurement and third-party risk reconcile this register with invoices, identity grants, gateway records and software inventory. A feature discovered without an invoice still needs an owner. A group contract does not automatically approve every subsidiary's data transfer.

## Step 2: ask for evidence that changes a decision

| Check | Evidence to request and test | Decision and accountable function |
|---|---|---|
| Use of broker data | Service-specific terms covering prompts, outputs, files, embeddings, telemetry, feedback and abuse logs; verify tenant settings | Privacy/legal approve permitted purposes, retention and transfers; data owner approves classes of data |
| Isolation and access | Tenant isolation tests, support-access controls, independent assurance scope, relevant exceptions | Third-party risk and security assess; business owner accepts residual risk within authority |
| Agent privileges | Exact scopes, token storage, delegation, expiry, revocation and export logs | IAM and application owner enforce minimum access; refuse unexplained broad grants |
| Changes | Snapshot/version policy, alias changes, deprecations, subprocessor notices and rollback options | Platform/product owners require staged tests before production change |
| Security and recovery | Incident contacts, sample notice, evidence availability, tested restore and dependency failures | SOC/IR and continuity teams test their interfaces, not just read a certificate |
| Viability and exit | Financial/operational viability, licensing rights, export sample, transition support and deletion process | Procurement, legal and business continuity decide whether dependence is acceptable |

A SOC 2 report for the parent company's office network is not evidence for the AI service's tenant boundary. Record the service, period, exclusions and unresolved findings. If evidence is unavailable, use synthetic/public data, remove write access, delay deployment or seek an authorised, expiring exception. Do not mark an unanswered question as a pass.

**Example:** a CRM assistant requests read/write access to all regional clients. The support owner needs drafts for one queue. IAM restricts access to that queue and removes send permission; the product team keeps sending in the existing human workflow. A promise that the model will only draft is not equivalent.

## Step 3: make obligations enforceable

Legal negotiates wording; procurement manages the supplier; privacy and compliance identify applicable local requirements. The following are negotiation fields, not universal legal clauses or regulatory deadlines.

| Contract schedule | Fields to settle before signature |
|---|---|
| Data and intellectual property | Permitted purpose; no training/reuse unless expressly authorised; ownership/licences for inputs, outputs and fine-tunes; confidentiality; prohibited data |
| Location and downstream parties | Approved processing/support locations; transfer mechanism; subprocessor list, notice, objection/remediation and termination rights |
| Change and security | Material-change definition; notice period; pinned-version availability; vulnerability response; access controls and evidence/audit rights |
| Incidents | Notice trigger, maximum elapsed notice time short enough for broker duties, 24×7 channel, minimum facts, rolling updates, evidence preservation and investigation cooperation |
| Availability | Service levels, dependency exclusions, recovery evidence, support escalation; credits are not a continuity plan |
| Exit | Export objects/formats and times, assistance price, transition window, deletion including downstream copies, backup expiry, attestation and verification rights |

For each promise record `clause ID / supplier contact / broker owner / technical check / evidence location / next test`. If storage is contractually disabled, platform operations still verifies configuration and the exact endpoint used. If a provider cannot pin a model, the product owner must treat drift as a monitored change and bound exposure; the contract must not be represented as technical pinning.

## Step 4: connect commercial review to the model supply chain

Procurement approves a relationship. It does **not** approve every model weight, adapter, prompt or tool released by that supplier. Existing software supply-chain owners operate the release controls, with model engineering and AI security adding behaviour tests.

| Artifact | Release record and gate | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Hosted model | Supplier/endpoint, exact snapshot or alias limitation, region/options, baseline eval result, rollback route | Model/platform owner |
| Open weights, adapters, tokenizers | Origin, licence, digest, signature/provenance where available, safe loader, vulnerability/malware checks | ML engineering and software supply-chain security |
| Dataset or retrieval corpus | Source rights, data classification, lineage, poisoning checks, access/deletion propagation | Data owner and retrieval team |
| Prompts, skills, MCP tools and connectors | Repository/version/digest, tool schema and scope diff, reviewed maintainer, approved registry entry | Application and platform owners |
| Packaged application | Linked artifact manifest, security eval version/results, approver, deployment ID | Product release owner |

Keep a machine-readable dependency manifest where possible. A digest proves which bytes were used, not that those bytes are safe. For a hosted model whose weights cannot be inspected, record that limitation; do not invent provenance. A changed connector, revoked licence or compromised maintainer triggers reassessment of affected releases, not merely a new annual questionnaire.

**Example:** an approved coding vendor ships an extension with changed tool descriptions. Endpoint engineering pauses rollout; software supply-chain security investigates the package; AI security tests whether the new instructions can reach secrets; IAM revokes exposed credentials if needed. The commercial approval remains evidence about the relationship, not permission to auto-update every laptop.

## Step 5: measure concentration and prove an exit

Two brands are not two independent routes if both use the same model host, cloud region or identity tenant. Link dependencies to critical business services and assess correlated loss, including legal restrictions, regional outages and licence withdrawal.

| Illustrative exposure | Why a second contract is insufficient | Proposed treatment and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 8 of 10 critical AI features use one model host | Resellers share its outage | Business continuity tests manual service; platform tests a separately approved route |
| Both regional deployments share a global gateway control plane | Region failover may retain the failed dependency | Platform documents failure domains and tests isolated operation |
| One KYC vendor is needed to open accounts | Unreviewed fallback can weaken identity checks | KYC operations tests controlled queueing/manual checks and acceptable backlog |

Report both feature counts and business impact: eight drafting tools are not eight settlement systems. Business owners set tolerances with continuity and operational risk. No automatic fallback to an unassessed model or another country; 01's failover rule still applies.

An exit rehearsal has an owner and a pass/fail record:

1. Export a representative authorised sample of configuration, histories, retrieval sources and audit metadata into an open format; validate completeness, permissions and usable reconstruction. Do not assume embeddings are portable.
2. Rebuild indexes and run functional/security evals on the proposed replacement. Reapprove data flows, access and model behaviour.
3. Route a limited cohort or use the manual workflow; reconcile records and account for in-flight jobs before cutting over. Measure time, cost and service backlog.
4. Revoke OAuth grants, API keys, webhooks, connector access and vendor support access; confirm no further traffic.
5. Obtain deletion evidence for active stores, subprocessors and scheduled backup expiry. Legal holds may require restricted retention, not deletion; record that exception and who owns it.

**Exit card:** `supplier / trigger / decision authority / services and entities / export scope and location / replacement or manual route / dependencies / migration estimate / cutover and rollback criteria / credential revoker / deletion evidence / test date and result / residual gaps and expiry`.

## Problem and next step

**Problem:** the supplier can be approved, pinned and contracted, yet a stolen token or poisoned update can still reach production. The broker needs to stop the service without losing evidence or accidentally repeating an order.
