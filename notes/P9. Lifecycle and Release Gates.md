# P9. Lifecycle and Release Gates

[P8. Governance and Global Operating Model](P8.%20Governance%20and%20Global%20Operating%20Model.md) named who may decide. Priya now has an approved diagram for the broker's order assistant. During implementation, a fallback model is added, raw prompts appear in a tracing service, and the confirmation screen drops the account number. The diagram is still approved. The system is different.

## The idea

**A gate approves a specific transition for a specific version and scope.** It is not a meeting, a slide deck or an approval of every future release. Evidence follows the system from intake to retirement.

These are **proposed company standards requiring adoption**. All examples and records below are illustrative. Nothing has been run, deployed, validated or certified. A checklist is not evidence that its control works. P2 determines security review depth; legal, privacy and impact-assessment triggers are separate.

## Step 1: define the release unit

A release is more than a model hash. Record the application build, model/provider snapshot, prompts, tool and MCP versions, identity scopes, data/index versions, retrieval permissions, output renderer, gateway policy, vendor settings, regional routes and human workflow. Where vendor versions cannot be pinned, record that limitation, change signals and the containment plan; do not pretend the dependency is fixed.

Ready-to-use gate record:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Feature, release and gate IDs | Stable inventory ID; candidate fingerprint; transition requested |
| Purpose and scope | Entities, regions, users, languages, tasks and exclusions |
| P2 facts and tier | Registry evidence; changes from last approved release |
| Legal and impact status | P10 obligation IDs; privacy/DPIA and responsible-AI assessment status |
| Evidence manifest | Test suite/version, environment, data conditions, results and limitations |
| Findings | Severity, exposure, owner, blocking status and retest evidence |
| Decision | Pass / conditional pass / hold / reject; rationale and permitted scope |
| Authority | Named approver and delegation; required specialist clearances |
| Operating conditions | Exposure caps, monitoring, expiry, stop triggers, rollback target |
| Status and timestamps | Not started / not run / run-failed / passed / inconclusive; date and author |

A conditional pass cannot cover a mandatory unmet legal condition or a failed hard safety boundary. It may cover a nonblocking improvement only where the authority schedule permits, with a bounded exception and expiry. An inconclusive required test is not a pass.

## Step 2: gate the lifecycle

The gate owner assembles evidence. The authority in P8 decides. Specialist clearance does not transfer ownership of the feature.

| Gate | Entry | Exit evidence and decision | Responsible owner | Stop condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G0: idea to investigation | Named sponsor, proposed purpose | Inventory ID, business owner, affected people, initial P2 facts and legal/impact screening | Product/business | No owner or unresolved prohibited-use concern: no build with live data |
| G1: design to build | Purpose and draft data flow | Threat model at required depth; regional annex; selected controls; oversight design; vendor/contract review plan; assessment requirements | Engineering lead | Unknown data route, unjustified scope or unresolved legal basis: restrict to synthetic design work |
| G2: build to controlled pilot | Implemented candidate | Component/integration tests, permission boundaries, minimised telemetry, model evaluations, authorised pilot cohort, approved data use, rollback and incident runbooks | Product engineering | Missing mandatory assessment or failed isolation: no pilot with affected users/data |
| G3: pilot to production | Pilot evidence and frozen candidate | Independent validation where required; relevant security and outcome tests; local clearances; staffed oversight; stop/rollback drill; residual-risk decision | Business release authority | Required test not run, unresolved blocker, absent operator or authority: hold |
| G4: operate and change | Approved production scope | Monitoring, incidents/complaints, periodic review, drift checks, expiring-exception decisions and change classification | Service owner | Stop trigger, expired critical clearance or boundary drift: contain affected scope |
| G5: retire and close | Retirement sponsor and dependency map | Access revoked; queued work stopped; data/retention/hold decisions executed; vendor evidence; customer handover; closure review | Service owner | Unknown residual copy or dependency: remain open as an owned retirement task |

Low-tier work can use automated evidence and delegated approval; it does not need a committee at every row. Tier 3 requires P2's deep review and red-team work. Under this proposal, tier 3 and material consequential uses also require independent validation before first production release and after material changes. The company must adopt the scope, competent reviewer pool and risk-based review cadence; a regulator or internal model-risk policy may require more.

## Step 3: make human oversight meaningful

A button labelled “approve” is not a control if the person cannot understand, refuse or stop the action. Nor does a person's involvement automatically make every outcome lawful.

| Oversight question | Order assistant's required design/evidence | A failing design |
|---|---|---|
| Can the person see the actual decision? | Exact account, instrument, buy/sell, quantity, order type, price conditions, charges where relevant and mandate used | “Proceed with recommended trade?” |
| Can they assess it? | Source and age of price/news, uncertainty and limitations; relevant account context without another client's data | Confident summary with no source or freshness |
| Can they choose freely? | Reject/edit/cancel; safe manual route; no penalty for escalation | Default auto-approval after a short timeout |
| Can they stop execution? | Confirmation bound to exact payload, identity and short validity; changed payload invalidates it | Model rewrites quantity after approval |
| Do they have competence and capacity? | Trained reviewer, realistic queue limits, measured review time, breaks and escalation | One reviewer expected to approve hundreds of complex decisions per hour |
| Can the outcome be challenged? | Accessible human support, correction/appeal route and audit trail | “The AI decided” closes the ticket |

For an unattended morning watcher, do not claim transaction-by-transaction human approval. Oversight is mandate setup, bounded code-enforced execution, monitoring, intervention and revocation. Validate whether that arrangement is permitted and sufficient for the specific use. If immediate intervention is needed but nobody is staffed, reduce autonomy or do not operate during those hours.

### An oversight test card

- [ ] Participant and role are representative; no real client harm is possible.
- [ ] Seed a wrong instrument, stale source, changed quantity and conflicting account context.
- [ ] Observe whether the reviewer notices, rejects, corrects and escalates; record misses as well as successes.
- [ ] Test a forged/replayed approval and payload changes after confirmation.
- [ ] Test accessibility, relevant language, peak load, handover and absent primary operator.
- [ ] Measure detection, decision and stop time against pre-agreed tolerances.
- [ ] Record staffing assumptions, failure thresholds, result, limitations and owner.

**Status for this example: not run.** Do not count the checklist as a completed oversight study.

## Step 4: validate independently, not ceremonially

Developers own normal testing. Independent validators challenge whether the use is fit for purpose under its stated conditions. They need competence in the domain and failure modes, access to failed results and configurations, and freedom to publish adverse findings. An external supplier who built the feature is not independent merely because it is external.

Minimum proposed validation report fields:

| Section | What to record |
|---|---|
| Mandate and independence | Reviewer, reporting route, prior involvement, conflicts and mitigation |
| Scope | Release fingerprint, regions/languages, use and excluded claims |
| Methods | Independent sampling, fresh attacks, business boundary and human-workflow tests |
| Data | Provenance, permission, representativeness, protected data handling and known gaps |
| Criteria | Pre-agreed tolerances and hard failure conditions, approved before results |
| Results | Numerators/denominators, uncertainty, subgroup/language results where appropriate, failures and limitations |
| Findings | Severity, exposure, reproducibility, owner, fix, retest and unresolved dissent |
| Conclusion | Fit / fit only within conditions / not fit / insufficient evidence; review triggers |

Cover security, performance and relevant harms. A prompt-injection score alone does not validate suitability for investment support. A fairness score alone does not establish permission isolation. Reuse evidence with provenance; independently challenge the important assumptions rather than rerunning every developer test by rote. Internal audit later assesses whether this process works; it is not G3's routine validator.

## Step 5: agree release thresholds before testing

| Test class | Example acceptance rule to agree before the run | Result in these notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hard authorisation | No tested path reads another client's account or places an order outside code-enforced limits; any violation blocks | Not run |
| Exact confirmation | Replayed, expired or changed-payload approvals are rejected in specified cases | Not run |
| Adaptive attacks | Report attack success and hard-control containment separately; compare with approved baseline and agreed tolerance | Not run |
| Business quality | Pre-agreed error tolerances on representative tasks, languages and critical failure cases | Not run |
| Human oversight | Reviewers detect seeded material errors and stop within the agreed operational bound | Not run |
| Regional routing | Normal, error, fallback, logs and support flows match the approved annex | Not run |
| Stop/rollback | Revoke identities, drain or cancel queues and restore an approved safe state; measure last possible action | Not run |

These are example rules, not invented test results or universal numeric thresholds. The owner and reviewers must set measurable tolerances for the use before running tests. Passing a finite suite does not prove zero risk.

## Step 6: a worked release decision

Priya proposes `orders:place` for the broker assistant. It reads client holdings and external news. P2 makes it tier 3 even when Asha confirms each order. The global model approval is not the release approval.

1. G1 records account isolation, `check_order`, exact confirmation, output limits and the worst case if the model is controlled.
2. G2 tests the complete payload and identities in a simulator, not with live investor orders.
3. G3 requires independent challenge of permissions, stale news, forged approval and reviewer behaviour, plus local clearance.
4. **Hypothetical decision:** if changing quantity after confirmation succeeds, hold release. Fix payload binding and retest affected paths; a business signature cannot replace the boundary.
5. If a regional privacy assessment is still open, keep that region off. Passing India tests does not establish approval elsewhere.
6. The actual status in this note remains **not run; no launch authorised**.

## Step 7: changes reopen the right gates

| Change | Proposed treatment |
|---|---|
| New tool/scope, data source, audience, region, autonomy or output destination | Recompute P2; reopen design and affected legal/impact assessments; review and validate changed boundaries |
| Model snapshot, prompt, retrieval/reranker or guardrail change | Rerun relevant security and quality evaluations; assess materiality and need for independent revalidation even if tier is unchanged |
| Backup provider or region | Treat as a release path, not an outage exception; pre-clear terms, routes and evaluations, or fail closed |
| UI confirmation, staffing or reviewer target changes | Reassess meaningful oversight and retest affected workflow |
| Vendor silently changes model/permissions | Compare observed behaviour/configuration, contain uncertainty, assess and revalidate; disable affected capability if boundaries cannot be assured |
| Emergency fix | Named emergency authority, smallest scope, evidence and rollback; time-bound retrospective review. No bypass of mandatory legal requirements |

Monitor tools, identities, data/index lineage, quality, harms, complaints and drift, not only model outputs. Every alert needs a staffed destination and a defined action. Reviews reopen on incidents, material complaints, control failures, changed law, vendor terms and expired acceptance as well as technical releases.

## Step 8: decommission is a release gate too

Turning off the UI does not retire an agent that still has a queue and a token.

- [ ] Name retirement owner, scope, date, dependencies and user/customer fallback.
- [ ] Stop new requests and schedules; cancel or safely drain in-flight jobs; reconcile partial orders/actions.
- [ ] Revoke app keys, service identities, delegated grants, MCP access, webhooks, secrets and vendor subscriptions as appropriate.
- [ ] Remove routes, tools, public endpoints, DNS references and marketplace entries; verify no remaining calls or spend through defined observation checks.
- [ ] Inventory prompts, traces, caches, indexes/embeddings, memories, uploads, evaluation data, fine-tunes, exports, backups and vendor/subprocessor copies.
- [ ] Apply P10's retention/hold/deletion decisions per data class; remove derived data and block restoration from reviving deleted material.
- [ ] Record what is deleted, retained, restricted, pending or not technically retrievable; include reason, owner and evidence.
- [ ] Obtain vendor deletion/retention evidence for the relevant account and copies; log gaps rather than asserting complete erasure.
- [ ] Preserve required decision/audit evidence with minimised personal data and controlled access.
- [ ] Test the fallback and confirm that dependent workflows no longer call the retired service.
- [ ] Seek closure review against evidence; unresolved residual copies remain assigned tasks, not a green “done”.

If data entered model training, deletion from the source or vector index does not prove removal from model weights. Escalate to privacy/legal and the supplier, assess available remediation, and state limits honestly.

## OWASP risks in this note

Using the course's 2026 category names; all examples are hypothetical:

- **LLM03: Excessive Agency.** Example: quantity changes after Asha approves. Issue: ceremonial oversight. Fix: exact payload binding and tested rejection of changes/replays.
- **LLM04: Supply Chain.** Example: outage failover selects an untested model. Issue: the release unit omitted backup paths. Fix: evaluated, cleared fallback or fail closed.
- **LLM02: Sensitive Information Disclosure.** Example: a retired copilot leaves client embeddings and vendor traces behind. Issue: retirement covered only the UI. Fix: lineage-based deletion and retained-copy evidence.
- **ASI10: Rogue Agents.** Example: a hidden scheduled job keeps acting after shutdown. Issue: no queue/identity closure. Fix: revoke, drain and verify the last possible action.

**Problem:** gates can demand “legal cleared” and “DPIA complete” without anyone knowing what those statements cover. A security tier cannot answer which law applies, who is responsible for processing, or where a vendor's support staff can see client data.
