# 13. People Funding and Assurance

The broker's AI security lead has thirty launches on the roadmap, two reviewers and a request to cover every region around the clock. The board has approved a gateway licence but no engineers to operate it. Every project is marked urgent. This is not a technical design problem. It is an operating model with demand, capacity and accountability missing.

## The idea

Define the service, cost the work and separate doing from checking. AI security should make the existing enterprise functions better at their jobs, not become the owner of every process containing the letters AI.

This note is a proposed operating model. All volumes, service targets, costs and thresholds are illustrative, not implemented staffing or contractual commitments. 03 explains what to measure; this note explains who produces, challenges and funds the evidence.

## Step 1: publish a service catalog

The catalog says what a team receives and what it must supply. The review SLAs below are **illustrative internal planning targets**, measured in the agreed regional business calendar, not 24×7 guarantees. The clock starts when minimum evidence is complete; separately report calendar time from initial request so “waiting for evidence” cannot hide delay. Pauses need an owner, reason and timestamp. A target is for a substantive response or decision, not guaranteed approval.

| Service and accountable provider | Minimum request fields | Illustrative target | Output and boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intake/routing — AI security with product owner | Entity, purpose, data, users, tools/actions, owner, launch date | Acknowledge in 2 business days; route/tier in 5 | Tier and control path; product owner remains responsible for truthful inventory |
| Tier 2 design review — trained product champion | Data flow, template deviations, cases and evidence | First substantive review in 5 business days | Decision/checklist; specialist escalation for novel exposure |
| Tier 3 specialist review — AI security | Threat model, permissions, model/tool manifest, evals, rollback, business exposure | First substantive review in 10 business days; aim for disposition in 20 after complete evidence | Findings, conditions or escalation; no promise to approve an unsafe design |
| Supplier assessment — enterprise third-party risk | 11 record, contract/data flow, supplier evidence | Route in 5 business days; joint assessment plan in 10 | Due-diligence decision; legal owns terms and procurement owns purchasing |
| Security evaluation — product engineering with AI security challenge | Approved test scope, safe accounts, release candidate, cases | Scope/schedule response in 5 business days | Reproducible results and release findings; product fixes its feature |
| Exception — business risk authority with risk/compliance | Exposure, alternatives, compensating controls, owner, expiry | Route in 2 business days; decision target agreed by authority | Time-limited acceptance or refusal, never silent approval |
| Incident — enterprise SOC/IR | Impact, affected IDs, times, available evidence | Existing 24×7 paging policy; 12 severity targets only if formally adopted | Command and response; not the design-review queue |

Publish contacts, escalation path, regional calendar, queue status and coverage exclusions. At risk of missing a target, the service lead offers a date and escalates capacity/risk to the accountable sponsor; teams do not gain a release waiver because a review is late. Fast-track only a preapproved unchanged pattern with recorded evidence. Emergency fixes use existing emergency change authority and retrospective review.

## Step 2: put ownership where the work is

| Function | Owns | Does not inherit from AI security |
|---|---|---|
| Product and business service owners | Features, business rules, client impact, test cases and residual risk within delegated authority | Accountability for unsafe outputs or actions |
| Platform/SRE and IAM | Gateway operations, reliability, scoped identity, revocation and production access | A free 24×7 service from reviewers |
| SOC/IR, fraud and continuity | Detection/response, fraud processes, business recovery respectively | A new AI-only command chain |
| Data owners and privacy | Access, classification, lawful use and data-subject obligations | Blanket approval from a model score |
| Procurement, third-party risk and legal | Supplier lifecycle, commercial process and contracts | Technical approval of every new model artifact |
| AI security specialists | Security standards, specialist reviews, adversarial methods and technical advice | Enterprise risk acceptance, legal interpretation or every operational control |
| HR and engineering leadership | Role-specific training, staffing and protected champion time | Unfunded volunteer capacity |

Train by role and test performance: a product champion reviews a scoped design; an on-call engineer demonstrates revocation; a procurement lead spots an unlisted model subprocessor; payment staff follow known-channel verification despite a convincing voice. Attendance is an input, not assurance. Champions need a named deputy, competence checks and paid allocation in their team's plan. Regional regulatory knowledge and language coverage are planned capabilities, not reasons to copy client data into a global chat.

## Step 3: calculate capacity before promising dates

Use hours of specialist work, not a count of reviews. This worked example is one steady-state month; the inputs are invented.

| Monthly specialist demand | Arithmetic | Hours |
|---|---|---:|
| Tier 3 reviews | 8 × 20 hours | 160 |
| Tier 2 escalations | 12 × 4 hours | 48 |
| AI-specific supplier assessments | 4 × 6 hours | 24 |
| Threat intelligence, eval upkeep and drills | Planned block | 64 |
| Metrics, standards and champion coaching | Planned block | 40 |
| **Planned demand** | 160 + 48 + 24 + 64 + 40 | **336** |

For one specialist: 160 paid hours/month minus 24 leave/holidays/training minus 24 administration/meetings = **112 allocatable hours**. Protect 20% of that for incident spikes and unplanned work: 112 × 0.80 = **89.6 planned hours**. Required specialists = 336 ÷ 89.6 = **3.75**, so propose **4**, subject to skill and arrival-pattern checks.

Four supply 358.4 planned hours and 89.6 protected hours across the team. The 22.4-hour planned surplus is not a fifth person's capacity. With only three specialists, planned capacity is 268.8 hours: **67.2 hours short per month**. If an inherited backlog is 120 hours and the sponsor wants it cleared over three months, add 40/month: demand becomes 376, and four are still **17.6 hours short/month**. Decide to defer launches, buy time-bounded competent help, reduce avoidable demand with templates, or fund more capacity. Do not solve the gap by removing all incident reserve.

Sensitivity: ten tier 3 reviews instead of eight add 40 hours, taking steady-state demand to 376; four no longer suffice at this allocation. Measure actual handling time and rework, then reforecast monthly. Split specialist skills and regional calendars: four interchangeable boxes do not guarantee four qualified reviewers. Four daytime specialists are also **not** a 24×7 rota. Enterprise SOC/SRE supplies existing incident coverage, with funded specialist escalation and deputies agreed separately. SLA feasibility depends on queue peaks and dependencies as well as average hours; validate it over a pilot period before commitment.

## Step 4: fund the whole control, not just the licence

The following annual incremental budget is illustrative, in USD for comparability across the MNC. Finance must replace rates with local loaded costs, exchange assumptions, taxes and approved allocations. Reused enterprise services still have capacity and cost, even when not charged to this program.

| Budget line | Assumption | Annual USD |
|---|---|---:|
| Specialist staff | 4 × $180,000 loaded annual cost | 720,000 |
| Protected product champion time | 10 × 0.1 FTE × $150,000 | 150,000 |
| Platform/SRE allocation | 1 FTE equivalent × $180,000 | 180,000 |
| Gateway, eval infrastructure and evidence storage | Incremental operating envelope | 120,000 |
| External independent testing | Scoped engagements | 80,000 |
| Role-based training and recovery exercises | Planned delivery costs | 30,000 |
| **Recurring subtotal** | Sum above | **1,280,000** |
| Contingency | 10% of recurring subtotal | 128,000 |
| **Recurring envelope** | Subtotal + contingency | **1,408,000** |
| One-time migration/integration | Separately governed project | 120,000 |
| **Illustrative year-one envelope** | Recurring envelope + one-time | **1,528,000** |

This is not the broker's entire AI bill: model inference, product development, core SOC, privacy/legal and business continuity base budgets are excluded. Confirm their incremental demand and funding with those owners; do not count the same FTE twice. Finance tracks committed, actual and forecast spend by cost centre. Contingency needs release authority, not automatic consumption.

Carry a rolling three-year view without pretending the first year's estimate is a fixed global run rate:

| Planning year | Scope and funding decision | Reforecast basis |
|---|---|---|
| Year 1: baseline and migration | Establish inventory, priority controls and measured service demand; separate the $120,000 illustrative migration project from recurring costs | Validate actual handling time, local loaded rates, integration effort and evidence-storage volumes |
| Year 2: regional expansion | Extend approved patterns where local entity needs justify them; fund local legal/privacy assessment, languages, working hours and operating support | Region-specific labour, currency, data-location, vendor and transfer costs; add capacity only against measured demand and required skills |
| Year 3: refresh, revalidation and exit | Revalidate critical suppliers and controls, refresh model/eval infrastructure, rehearse exit and replace unsupported components | Contract renewals, deprecations, retention growth, dependency concentration and tested migration effort; retain transition funding where required |

Finance and service owners rebaseline this view at least annually. Do not assume automatic savings, flat local costs or blanket headcount growth; record assumptions and downside scenarios explicitly.

Each investment card records `risk/exposure / affected services and entities / control owner / build and run cost / staffing dependency / milestone / metric expected to move / evidence of benefit / alternatives / deferrals and risk acceptance`. Example: fund IAM integration to remove standing vendor grants, measured by scoped/revocable-grant coverage and drill results. Do not justify it with fictional “breaches prevented × average breach cost”.

## Step 5: separate three lines of assurance

Apply the enterprise's three-lines model, not three AI committees. Security teams can straddle lines; make the role in each activity explicit and preserve independent challenge.

| Line | AI application | Evidence and independence |
|---|---|---|
| First line: business, product and operating control owners | Build/run controls, manage suppliers, classify features, test releases, fix findings and own risk | Deployment manifests, evals, access reviews, incident/drill records; specialist engineering help is still first-line implementation |
| Second line: independent enterprise risk/compliance and designated security oversight | Set/challenge policy, question tiers and risk acceptances, sample evidence and escalate systemic gaps | Risk-based sampling, exception challenge and reporting to risk authority; do not self-approve controls they built |
| Third line: internal audit | Independently assess governance and control design/operation against an audit plan | Reports to audit governance; independently selected samples and follow-up; does not operate controls or approve releases |

External assessors may support any line; buying a penetration test does not create third-line independence. If the AI security team operates the gateway policy and also reports on its effectiveness, have separate enterprise risk oversight or another suitably independent function challenge it. Internal audit decides its own scope. Business owners accept risk only within delegated limits; legal prohibitions and mandatory requirements are not waivable through an AI exception form.

A useful assurance sample traces one tier 3 feature end to end: inventory → supplier/data flow → threat model → release evidence → production policy → actual grant revocation → risk decision. Sample changed and exception-bearing features as well as convenient compliant ones. Findings need `severity / exposed service / accountable owner / due date / interim control / validation owner / closure evidence / overdue escalation`. Closure requires a retest or other verified evidence, not the ticket author's assertion.

## Step 6: keep a metrics dictionary

A metric needs a population and clock before it needs a dashboard. 03's 100% coverage goals remain ambitions, not a statement of current compliance. The thresholds below are proposed management triggers only. “Unknown” is a visible state, never silently counted as compliant; a zero denominator is **N/A**, not 100%.

| Metric and exact definition | Source, owner and cadence | Illustrative action trigger / interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory coverage: active discovered features with current intake ÷ all active discovered features; include vendor AI and regional deployments under a documented counting rule | Reconciled gateway, SaaS/IAM and purchasing records; inventory owner; monthly | Goal 100%; list every gap and discovery-source limitations—this cannot prove no shadow AI exists |
| Tier 3 control coverage: active tier 3 features with **all** required current evidence ÷ all active tier 3 features; report each component too | Release/eval/kill-switch records; control owners; monthly | Goal 100%; any missing hard boundary triggers owner escalation, not averaging away the gap |
| Review timeliness: eligible reviews receiving substantive response within catalog target ÷ eligible reviews responded to; also show all open overdue items | Intake timestamps, pause log and regional calendar; service lead; weekly | Below proposed 90% triggers capacity review; report median/p90 initial-to-response and complete-to-response times, sample size and ageing |
| Unsafe-action rate: test runs in which an unauthorised data disclosure/action actually reaches a destination ÷ valid attack runs | Versioned cases, release ID and destination assertions; product tester; every release | Any critical boundary failure blocks release; separate fixed/fresh suites and model compliance from actual action success |
| Time to stop: elapsed time from containment decision to last successful prohibited action, verified at destinations | Decision log, IAM/tool/destination logs; IR; each drill/incident | Proposed goal ≤5 minutes for scoped isolation; report every result and maximum, not only average; ongoing ability is “not yet stopped” |
| Recovery compliance: rehearsals meeting both agreed RTO and RPO ÷ completed in-scope rehearsals; list untested services separately | BIA targets, restore timestamps and reconciliation; continuity/service owners; quarterly | Any critical failure or overdue test gets a funded remediation decision; tabletop alone is not restore evidence |
| Supplier critical-dependency exposure: critical business services depending on each shared host/region ÷ all critical business services using AI | 11 dependency map and BIA; third-party risk/continuity; quarterly | Review any single dependency exceeding proposed 50%; report business impact and tested alternatives, not just counts |
| Overdue risk/finding count: open items past agreed expiry/due date as of reporting cutoff, with age and severity | Risk/finding registers; risk oversight; monthly | Goal zero overdue critical items; extensions retain original age and require authorised decision |
| Forecast capacity gap: planned demand hours − staffed planned capacity hours; show assumptions and backlog separately | Time samples, staffing and intake forecast; service lead/finance; monthly | Positive gap requires prioritisation/funding decision; overtime is not assumed permanent capacity |

Store with each metric `definition version / reporting cutoff in UTC / entity and scope / numerator / denominator / exclusions / missing data / source query / evidence link / producer / challenger / trend / decision`. Retain historical definitions or restate trends explicitly when the population changes. Pair speed with safety: faster reviews accompanied by more escaped critical findings are not success.

## Step 7: make the board choose

A quarterly page should show 03's coverage/effectiveness/exposure view, top business-service risks, tested recovery gaps and the decisions required. Add demand/capacity and forecast funding only where they explain a decision.

**Decision template:** `decision requested / options and cost / exposure if deferred / accountable business owner / recommendation / authority required / deadline / approved action / conditions and expiry / evidence due at next review`.

For example: “Three specialists leave 67.2 planned hours/month uncovered. Fund a fourth, reduce launch demand, or explicitly defer named reviews and launches. No release bypass is implied.” Keep the deferred-work list visible. Management may change priorities; it cannot create engineering capacity by changing a dashboard to green.

## Problem and next step

**Problem:** a documented program can still be a proposal with no operating evidence. The final test is whether named owners can execute it with their funded capacity and whether another line can verify the result.
